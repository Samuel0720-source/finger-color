Letter from the author

I started finger-color with a simple question: can the camera that is already on a laptop tell someone whether their hand symptoms are worth showing to a doctor? Raynaud's, trigger finger and carpal tunnel are common, they are easy to describe badly in a short appointment, and each has a visible sign that a camera might catch.

Building the app was the easy half. The harder half was finding out whether its numbers can be trusted. So before measuring anyone, I wrote down what "good enough" means: the bend angle within ±5° on average, every single reading within ±15°, at least 80% of deliberate catches found, and so on. Those rules are printed on page 12 and have not been changed since.

This pilot measured {{people}} people for trigger finger and began the Raynaud's color test on one. Some rules passed and some did not. The middle finger never raised a false alarm in {{fa}} smooth fists, and the angle is right on average ({{bias}}). But a fully straight finger reads too bent, the thumb raises false catches, and one volunteer's catches were missed entirely.

I am reporting the failures as clearly as the passes, because they are the most useful part. Two of them led to real fixes already: fists are now counted from each person's own open hand, which turned 0 of 10 counted fists into 10 of 10 for one volunteer, and held fists no longer count as catches on the little finger. The rest are listed as decisions to make before the next study.

Thank you to the friends who sat still for 25 minutes while I read a goniometer off their fingers.

Samuel Koo
Author, and participant P01 / R01

The product

A browser app that measures the hand with the camera and compares the result with published clinical criteria.

Part I · The productItem 1Item 1A
ITEM 1

What the app does

finger-color runs in the browser. The video never leaves the device: hand tracking (MediaPipe, 21 points per frame) and all measurements happen locally.

Three checks

  • Raynaud's: reads the color of each fingertip against the person's saved normal color, and times how long the color takes to return.
  • Trigger finger: measures the angle at the middle joint of each finger during 10 fists, and flags a catch: a pause in mid-bend followed by a quick snap open.
  • Carpal tunnel: checks whether the thumb tip reaches each fingertip.

What the person gets

A short questionnaire plus the camera measurements are compared with each condition's clinical criteria. The result is one of a few plain next steps, a PDF to bring to a doctor, and the raw data as CSV files.

How it is built

Vite and plain JavaScript; MediaPipe Hands in WebAssembly; Chart.js and jsPDF; Python (pandas, matplotlib) for validation; 97 automated tests and a browser test with a simulated hand on every change; deployed on Vercel.

ITEM 1A

Limits and risks

Who was measured

Three healthy adults for trigger finger and one for Raynaud's, all friends of the author or the author himself. None has the conditions the app screens for, so this pilot cannot say whether the app detects them. It can only say how accurately it measures.

One camera, one room

All sessions used one laptop camera in normal indoor light. Light, distance and skin tone may change the results and have not been tested yet.

The reference is a person

The author read both the app and the goniometer for every pair. A goniometer read by hand is itself off by a few degrees.

Known measurement problems

  • Straight finger: with the palm toward the camera, a fully straight finger read {{smin}} to {{smax}} too bent in the {{nstraight}} straight pairs.
  • Thumb: the thumb raises false catches in every hand tested. It is still shown in the result.
  • Hands that do not open far: two of three hands opened only to about 142°, which leaves little room for the 25° snap a catch needs.
  • Color in a changing scene: during a still recording, all five fingers jumped together twice, which points to light or camera exposure, not skin.
The app is not a medical device. It does not diagnose. Its result is a suggestion about whether to see a doctor, and it says so on every screen and in every PDF.

ITEM 7 · RAYNAUD'S

The camera sees a pale fingertip, but cannot time it yet

Why no press was timed

  • Five recordings were stopped 55 to 59 s after letting go; the app needs 60 s and does not say when that point is reached.
  • One ran 68 s, but the recovery line (1.7, from the few seconds used to save the normal color) is stricter than the real noise of a still hand in this room.

ITEM 9

How the study was run

Rules first, data second

Each test has a written protocol with pass or fail rules fixed before any measurement. Protocols are versioned; a change is allowed only before the data it affects is taken, and the reason is recorded.

Nothing removed, nothing invented

Every run is kept, including the ones that failed or were done the wrong way. Practice recordings outside the protocol are kept in the raw folder and labelled. No number was typed in that was not measured.

Privacy

Volunteers are adults who read a one-page description and agreed before starting, are known only by a code (P01, R01) and can stop at any time. No video or face is stored.

ITEM 10

What comes next

  • Decide how to read a straight finger (side view, or each person's straight hand as zero), designed on some people and tested on others.
  • A thumb rule that removes false catches without dropping real ones, or leave the thumb out of the result.
  • Raynaud's: a 60-second countdown after "I'm warming up now", and a recovery line based on a real still recording.
  • Full study: 10 people, 3 days each, two light conditions; this is where repeatability is measured.
