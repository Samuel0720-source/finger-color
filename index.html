    Observe color as you warm up

    For an ordinary, naturally occurring color change, start recording and mark when warming begins. Keep your hand in view for at least 60 seconds afterward. Do not deliberately cool your hands for this test.

Research option: before recording, tap a photo grey card in the video to export corrected color values. The recovery score always uses the palm reference.

Finger catching

Palm toward the camera, whole hand in view. Slowly make a fist, then open it all the way. Repeat 10 times. The test stops by itself.

Start the camera first.

This explores pauses followed by quick opening. Camera estimates cannot confirm or rule out a condition. Stop if a movement hurts. The app tracks four fingers (not the thumb). Progress follows the middle finger. Allow about 20–60 seconds. A flag is a movement pattern, not proof of locking.

Thumb tapping

Keep thumb and index fingertips visible without overlap. First hold them touching, then apart to calibrate. After the countdown, touch and separate them repeatedly for 10 seconds. You are tapping your fingers together, not tapping the screen.

How it works

  1. Find the hand

    A hand-tracking model (Google MediaPipe) finds 21 points on your hand in every video frame, both on the picture and in 3D.

  2. Measure

    Color is converted to CIELAB, a scale built around human vision, and compared with your palm to reduce some lighting effects. Camera exposure, skin tone, position, and tracking quality can still affect results. 3D landmarks estimate movement; they are not a calibrated clinical measurement.

  3. Score

    Recovery time after cold, the number of catches, and taps per second with rhythm steadiness. Review your own history using the same hand, camera, and lighting. Changes in setup can change the result.

Privacy

Video is analyzed on your device and never uploaded. Your normal color and history are stored only in this browser. Clearing your browser data removes them.

Not a medical device

This is a personal research tool built as a student project. It does not diagnose any condition. Share your report with a clinician if something worries you.
